Dame Whina Cooper

"Mother of the Nation" is an honorary title given to Dame Whina Cooper because she was the main driver for the Māori Land March in 1975. Though at the age of 79 with painful arthritis, she still had the energy and determination to lead the hīkoi. Through her, the formation of Te Rōpū o te Matakite brought together all iwi and hapū to rally against the dispossession of Māori land to the Crown through one strong symbol.

While leading the exhausting journey of 1,000 kilometers from Te Hāpua to Wellington, her tactful approach made it inevitable for all to talk about the march due to the cultural and political differences involved. She made sure everyone knew that the hīkoi was a non-violent effort to preserve the spirit, identity, and future of the Māori lands.

The picture of Whina Cooper walking hand-in-hand with her small granddaughter right at the front of the march will go down as one of the most memorable and unforgettable images in New Zealand's history. Whina Cooper's great commitment during the march changed everything about the country's politics, as it showed the government how peaceful protest and cultural solidarity can make the government address the issue of past land confiscations. In today's context, the 1975 march led by Whina Cooper is one of the most significant milestones in activism.
