Introduction

Born on 24th of May 1819 at Kensington Palace which was hidden away west of England Alexendria Victoria was raised there in isolation. When her elder cousins failed to thrive and died Victoria grew immensely important because she would be the one to inherit the throne therefore reigning over the British Isles, Ireland, and one quarter of the globe's population.

The death of Victorias Father

Queen Victoria's journey to the crown had not been clear at first especially when her education and training for the position of Queen was unsatisfactory. A problem contributing to this was the early loss of her father, the Duke of Kent. He left Victoria and her mother, Victoire, in massive debts and in his attempt to save money moved both his wife and infant daughter to live cheaply in a rented holiday house during the winter. It was there he caught Pneumonia and passed away.

Victoires Situation

The duchess of Kent, Victoire, was left on her own in a difficult situation where she lacked the ability to speak English and was practically excluded from the rest of the royal family. She had little resources, financial or otherwise, and yet the responsibility of raising Victoria was hers. In this case the child in line for the throne would be passed over to the current reigning monarch for education and guardianship but the Duke of Kent had entrusted his wife with that duty before he passed.

As the years went by and the King, King William IV, who was Victoria's uncle gradually worsened in health, it was a matter of time before he passed away and Victoria was still too young to ascend to the throne. At this point, it became a race to the crown between Victoria and her mother Victoire, if by the time the King had passed on and Victoria was still underaged for the position her mother would take up the throne as a ‘Regent’ - a temporary position until Victoria was old enough to claim the throne. However Victoire was German and spoke little to no English which means she would rule over a quarter of the world's population and multiple countries without speaking the common language. And historically there would be foul play with Regents getting rid of the child in line for the throne to keep the position as ruler, this was a common outcome when a Regent was needed because people would get too attached to the position to give it up so easily.
