Cyberbullying

What is Cyberbullying?


Cyberbullying is online harassment through messages, posts, comments, images, or just social media in general. It can include spreading rumours, excluding people online, or sending hurtful messages that can deeply affect someone's wellbeing.


Why It's Harmful:

It can be very harmful, affecting a person's confidence, mental health, and sense of safety. It may feel overwhelming, as it can also causes emotional pain, anxiety, and stress. Because it happens online, it can follow someone everywhere and feel impossible to escape, making it even more harmful. However, knowing how to respond can help protect your wellbeing. Everyone has the right to use the internet without fear of being bullied, and there are clear steps you can take to stay safe.

What You Can Do

  1. Block or report the person - Stop contact and report the behaviour on the platform.
  2. Save evidence (screenshots) - Keep proof of messages or posts in case you need to report it.
  3. Talk to a trusted adult - Tell a parent, teacher, or school counsellor so you can get support.
  4. Don't respond or engage - Responding can make the situation worse, so it's best to ignore it.
  5. Adjust your privacy settings - Limit who can contact or view your profile to stay safer online.
