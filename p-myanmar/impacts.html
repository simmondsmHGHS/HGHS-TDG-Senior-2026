Impact On Youth

Wellbeing Of Youth

Many young people in Myanmar experience fear and insecurity as part of everyday life. A UNDP survey of more than 7,000 young people found that one in three frequently experiences anxiety or stress. Many described living in “survival mode” because of conflict, financial hardship and concerns about their families’ safety.

Impact On Youth

Safety Risks

For many children going to school or playing outdoors can be dangerous. Between January and September 2024, approximately 250 children were killed or injured by landmines of civil war in Myanmar. Explosive contamination affected nearly every state and region. This put homes, playgrounds, fields and routes to school at risk. Children who are out of school face greater exposure to child labour, forced recruitment, trafficking and early marriage.

Impact On Youth

Limited Access

Learning continues but many students cannot access suitable teachers, materials or proper classrooms. UNICEF reports shown that 2/3 of children with disabilities are out of school. The teacher shortages risen up to 50%. Around 60% of out-of-school children are aged between 15-17. Rural, displaced and disabled learners experience barriers to safe and consistent education.

Impact On Youth

Uncertain Futures

Interrupted education lowers burmese young people’s future study and employment opportunities. Discovery from UNDP presents that more than five million young people aged round 18 to 24 were no longer participating in education or training. Around three million people, were not in employment, education. Without any qualifications, skills and stable work opportunities, many young people face long term financial insecurity. This also add pressure to leave Myanmar to find better opportunities..

Voices of Youth

Hope Of Returning Home

"I want peace to return swiftly so I can go back to my home."

Kyang Sau, 16years old was displaced with her family. They struggled with overcrowded living conditions, limited access to basic supplies and interruptes learning. While facing these challenges, her spirit is not broken. Her dream is to become a football player, She strongly support Manchester United. Her favourite player is Cristiano Ronaldo. However, she wishes the conflict to end which has overturned her dreams.

Dreams At Risks

“My dreams of overcoming our family's hardships are now at risk, without my leg to propel me forward,”

15 years old Aung had a dream of being a Soldier to protect people. After he had stepped on the landmine. He only dreams of being able to somehow support his parents, and his future family. His dream of being a soldier is forever gone.

Losing Direction

"I want to go to school, study hard and become a doctor my family can depend on,"

Thae Thae Su Aung, 14 years old has a goal of becoming a Doctor. She wanted to help her sister and mother who are frail and chronic illnesses. She has to stopped learning due to COVID-19 and Armed conflicts.
