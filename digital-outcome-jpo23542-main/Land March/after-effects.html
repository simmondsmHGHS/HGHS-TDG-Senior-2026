After-effects of the March

The after-effects of the march on legislation have been very dramatic and have changed the face of legislation in New Zealand considerably. Over the years since the march took place, the Crown has been involved in the Treaty Settlements Process, which has entailed apologizing to many iwi for past wrongdoings and providing money to cover for losses as well as providing business solutions for those losses. Most significantly, however, it has involved giving back culturally, spiritually and physically important land back to its rightful mana whenua owners.

Not limited to just the legal system and parliamentary debate, this hīkoi launched a cultural and political renaissance throughout the entire country of Aotearoa. Not only did this hīkoi demolish the illusion that New Zealand had perfected the relationship between all races, but it sparked a new generation of passionate youth who would fight for the cause of mana motuhake (self-determination). The unity that was formed during the journey because of whanaungatanga (connection) between different iwi was a catalyst to transform the indigenous movement. This 1975 hīkoi became the model for future protests in a very direct way by inspiring the following occupations.

The arrival of the Māori Land March in Wellington did not mark the end of anything but rather served as the beginning of change throughout all of Aotearoa. While Parliament did not immediately respond, the iconic image of so many people exercising mana motuhake could not help but shift the political rhetoric and galvanize a whole new generation of Māori activists. Immediately giving the newly formed Waitangi Tribunal, established only weeks ago but limited in scope, momentum towards future changes which would allow it to investigate past grievances back to 1840. Making the practice of the hīkoi one which was well-established as a tool of both social and political challenge, paving the way for further historic events, like the Seabed and Foreshore protests in 2004 and the occupation of Ihumātao.
