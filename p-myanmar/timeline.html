Myanmar's CIVIL WAR

TIMELINE

1948 onwards

Myanmar(Burma) has experienced armed conflict since independence in 1948. Multiple ethnic armed organisations fought the Tatmataw(military) for greater freedom, equality and political rights.The ongoing war has older roots. It did not begin only with the 2021 coup.

-Image by unknown author on Wikimedia Commons

2008

Myanmar’s 2008 Constitution reserved 25% of parliamentary seats for the Tatmataw(military). Constitution gave Tatmataw(military) control over important ministries. 75% votes are need to amend the 2008 Constitution. The military could block constitutional changes that threatened its power.

-Image By Prachatai on Flickr

1962–2011

In 2022, the conflict intensified. The military increased air strikes and burned down the villages. In the meantime, the resistance force became more organised and stronger. The civil war geographically expanded. The conflict has turned into a multi front war including formal alliances, independent armed groups and military aligned forces.

-Image by People's Pictorial via Wikimedia Commons

November 2020

The National League for Democracy won the election by a large margin. The military refused. The military accused that the election has been tainted by fraud and irregularities. There was no evidence of the fraud and irregularities of the election and the electoral commission rejected the request.

-Image by C.GEORGE via Wikimedia Commons

1 February 2021

By using fraud claim, the military declared to arrest civilian leaders and transferred the power to Min Aung Haling. The military arrested civilian leaders. It took control before the newly elected parliament was due to meet. The coup concluded Myanmar’s limited civilian government's period. This triggered nationwide opposition.

-Image by Vadim Savitsky / Mil.ru via Wikimedia Commons

February–September 2021

Burmese citizens responded with, strikes and the Civil Disobedience Movement. Military and police forces violently suppressed protests. Resistance groups began organising across Myanmar.

Photo by Saw Wunna on Unsplash

April–September 2021

Opponents of military rule formed the National Unity Government(NUG). The People’s Defence Force(PDF) was later formed as its armed resistance branch, while some groups cooperated with existing ethnic armed organisations. NUG and PDF fought against the military to protect the citizens in danger.

-Image by Ninjastrikers via Wikimedia Commons

2022

Conflicts geographically expanded into more towns, villages and central regions. The military increasingly used airstrikes, raids and village burnings. Resistance forces became more organised.

-Map by Ecrusized, derived from work by NordNordWest, via Wikimedia Commons

October 2023

The Three Brotherhood Alliance(coalition of three ethnic armed organisations) launched a major offensive in northern Shan State in Myanmar, capturing military positions, towns and transport routes. Related offensives also developed in other regions. The operation placed the military under major pressure and changed the balance of the conflict.

-Map by Ecrusized, derived from work by NordNordWest, via Wikimedia Commons

2024

Resistance groups gained territory in several regions. The military continues to control major cities and uses air power. Burmese civilians continue to face displacement, disrupted education and limited access to essential services. Neither side gained a decisive nationwide victory. The humanitarian crisis continues.

-Map by Ecrusized, derived from work by NordNordWest, via Wikimedia Commons

28 March 2025

A magnitude 7.7 Mandalay earthquake struck central Myanmar. This lead to damaged homes, hospitals, roads and other essential infrastructure. The disaster impacted communities already experiencing both war and displacement.

Although several armed groups announced a temporary suspension of fighting to support rescue work, military airstrikes continued.

-Image by China News Service via Wikimedia Commons

December 2025-January 2026

Myanmar’s military organised a three-stage election while conflicts continued across the country. The National League for Democracy was excluded. Aung San Suu Kyi remained house imprisoned. Voting was not taken place in many areas affected by conflict. The military-backed USDP won the election, while the military continued to hold constitutionally reserved seats in parliament. USDP: The Union Solidarity and Development Party, a political party closely connected to Myanmar’s military.

-Image by Vadim Savitsky / Mil.ru via Wikimedia Commons
